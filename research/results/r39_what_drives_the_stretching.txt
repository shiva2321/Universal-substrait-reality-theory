G1. A single barotropic medium cannot slow-roll: the identity, and the spectrum it makes instead
==============================================================================
Zero-temperature superfluid: pressure and energy depend on one number (mu), so P = P(rho).  Then d rho / dN = -3 (rho + P) gives
    d ln(rho + P)/dN = -3 (1 + c_s^2),   d ln rho / dN = -3 (1 + w)   =>   d ln eps / dN = -3 (c_s^2 - w)   [exact].
          bag c_inf^2 = 1.0: identity and Friedmann relation checked at four times, worst error 2.3e-10
          bag c_inf^2 = 0.5: identity and Friedmann relation checked at four times, worst error 1.4e-10
    chaplygin c_inf^2 = 1.0: identity and Friedmann relation checked at four times, worst error 3.4e-10
    chaplygin c_inf^2 = 0.5: identity and Friedmann relation checked at four times, worst error 1.2e-10
    Slow roll that gives n_s - 1 = -0.035 needs |eta| = |d ln eps/dN| <~ 0.0175: c_s^2 = w - eta/3 = -0.988 +- 0.006, i.e. a NEGATIVE
    c_s^2: perturbations grow as exp(|c_s| k t) (gradient instability), fastest at the cutoff k ~ 1/xi, i.e. within ~ one Planck time.
    (A scalar field with a potential is not barotropic: its adiabatic c_a^2 = -1 - 2V'/(3 H phidot) -> -1 in slow roll, while its
    physical sound speed is 1.  The gap is the non-adiabatic pressure; a one-component T = 0 medium has no such gap.)

What such a medium does instead.  Near its de Sitter fixed point eps ~ exp(-3 (1 + c^2) N) -- an ultra-slow-roll: zeta is NOT frozen
outside the sound horizon but grows as exp[(3 c^2) N].  Counting that growth, the spectrum is predicted analytically to be
    n_s - 1 = 3 (1 - c_inf^2),    c_inf = the sound speed at the fixed point.
Check by integrating the full mode equation  zeta'' + (3 - eps + eta - 2s) zeta' + (c_s k / aH)^2 zeta = 0  from the vacuum (modes
exiting 5.5-11 e-folds after acceleration starts, power read at N = 18):
                        medium  c_inf^2  n_s-1 measured  3(1-c_inf^2)  difference
                  constant c_s   0.3600          1.9200        1.9200     2.2e-05
                  constant c_s   0.6400          1.0800        1.0800    -1.5e-06
                  constant c_s   1.0000         -0.0000        0.0000    -3.3e-06
         Chaplygin gas (brane)   0.5000          1.5000        1.5000     6.3e-06
         Chaplygin gas (brane)   1.0000          0.0000        0.0000     4.3e-06
                  constant c_s   1.0117         -0.0351       -0.0351    -3.3e-06
    => the formula holds to 2e-05.  Planck's n_s = 0.9649 +- 0.0042 needs c_inf^2 = 1.0117 (2-sigma range 1.0089 to 1.0145):
       a sound speed ABOVE c.  A causal medium (c_s <= c, and c_s = c in the Planck-length mapping) gives n_s >= 1.  For c_s = c the
       spectrum is exactly flat (n_s = 1.0000), which is 8.4 sigma from the measurement.
    Tensors: gravitational waves freeze at exit while zeta keeps growing, so r = 16 eps c_s e^{-2(3 c^2) dN} is far below any limit.
    Besides, the fixed point is eternal: there is no end of stretching and no reheating in this medium.  It models dark energy, not an
    inflation that ends.  (Priors: ultra-slow-roll, Kinney 2005; Chaplygin gas, Kamenshchik-Moschella-Pasquier 2001; brane origin,
    Bilic-Tupper-Viollier 2002; kinetic-driven inflation, Armendariz-Picon-Damour-Mukhanov 1999.)

==============================================================================
G2. A stretched liquid: how far can tension go before the liquid tears (cavitates)?
==============================================================================
Causal bound.  Start at the equilibrium density rho_0 (P = 0, the vacuum of the liquid) and dilute it.  Since dP = c_s^2 d rho and
c_s <= c everywhere, P(rho) >= -(rho_0 - rho), so  w = P/rho >= -(rho_0/rho - 1).  w < -1/3 needs rho < (3/4) rho_0; w = -0.988 needs
rho < 0.50 rho_0 and c_s = c all the way down: the bag equation of state, whose sound speed never drops.  A real liquid's sound speed
falls to zero at the spinodal.
Real liquids (rest mass counts in rho): w_min >~ -(c_s/c)^2 x (stretch factor of order 1):
      He-4: c_s = 238 m/s  ->  (c_s/c)^2 = 6.3e-13: w stays within ~6e-13 of zero.
     water: c_s = 1480 m/s  ->  (c_s/c)^2 = 2.4e-11: w stays within ~2e-11 of zero.
    For the Planck-length medium c_s = c, so the bound allows w of order -1; the question is what the shape of P(rho) does.

Scan of shapes E(n) = -a n^q + b n^r (a, b fixed by P(1) = 0 and c_s(1) = c), requiring a stable branch with 0 < c_s^2 <= 1 between
n = 1 and the spinodal.  w and e-folds with w < -1/3 as n falls (n ~ a^-3):
    4900 (q, r) pairs tried; 3351 give a causal, stable stretched liquid with a spinodal.
    A catch found on the first pass: the most negative w sat in the corner q, r -> 1, where the binding energy per constituent
    approaches the whole rest-mass energy, rho_0 -> 0, and w = P/rho blows up for a trivial reason.  So the result is reported against
    the binding fraction f_b = -E(1)/(m c^2) (f_b = 0.4 for the quantum-droplet shape; liquid He-4, 7.2 K per atom, has f_b = 2e-13):

    laws only; it does not prove no liquid exists.  What it shows is that the causal bound w >= -(rho_0/rho - 1) is not approached.)
    The medium stretches, cavitates, and the cavities merge: after the spinodal the 'medium' is a foam, not an expanding fluid.

==============================================================================
G3. An open medium that makes new medium as space grows (reasoning, no number)
==============================================================================
If the density is held fixed by creating medium at a rate Gamma = 3 H per particle, rho = const, w = -1 exactly, de Sitter by
construction (steady state: Bondi-Gold-Hoyle 1948; irreversible creation pressure: Prigogine et al. 1988).  Two problems remain:
  (i) eps = 0 exactly, so the medium has no clock of its own: no scalar perturbation exists without a second ingredient;
  (ii) the creation rate is an input, and creating medium out of nothing is a statement about the hyper-dimensions that is not derived.
This is the 'space grows by itself' picture; it explains the stretch by assuming it.

==============================================================================
G4. A second sector: the medium's own phase field as a pseudo-Goldstone (natural inflation)
==============================================================================
The phase theta of the condensate is a free field (shift symmetry) with canonical decay constant F^2 = hbar^2 n / (m c_s^2).
In the r33 mapping (xi = alpha l_P = hbar/(sqrt2 m c), c_s = c) with n xi^3 the number of constituents in a healing volume (mean-field
needs n xi^3 >> 1): in Planck units F = 2^(1/4) sqrt(n xi^3)/alpha, and in units of the reduced Planck mass (m_P / sqrt(8 pi)):
    F / M_Pl = 5.96 sqrt(n xi^3) / alpha   (an estimate from the mapping, not a measurement; n xi^3 >= 1, alpha ~ 1).
A potential for the phase needs an explicit U(1)-breaking term (for example a Josephson coupling to a second condensate): V = L^4 (1 + cos(theta)),
which is natural inflation (Freese-Frieman-Olinto 1990).  Closed-form (n_s, r) at N e-folds before the end:

    r < 0.036 needs f <= 5.2 M_Pl (N = 60), where n_s = 0.9542 (2.6 sigma low).
    The medium's own F = 6.0 sqrt(n xi^3)/alpha M_Pl is at or above the r bound unless alpha >~ 1.1 sqrt(n xi^3).
    Natural inflation therefore sits at the edge of what the data allow, with the medium's F in the right order of magnitude (>~ 5 M_Pl is
    unusual for a field theory, and here it arrives for free).  But it needs a second sector, a potential nobody derived, and a medium
    with an explicit phase-breaking term; the amplitude (Lambda) is one free number.
