
==============================================================================
A. Is the lumpiness in the light, or only in the detector?
==============================================================================
Setup: heralded beam-splitter experiment (Grangier, Roger & Aspect 1986).  alpha = N_G N_TR / (N_T N_R);
alpha = 1 means the two detectors click independently, alpha < 1 means they avoid clicking together.
Every model below has the same mean click probability per detector (0.050) and dark counts (0.001);
4,000,000 gate windows each.
Classical wave of intensity I per window, clicks proportional to I: alpha = <I^2> / <I>^2 >= 1 (Cauchy-Schwarz),
whatever the intensity pattern; a smooth wave can never make the detectors avoid each other.

    model                                         alpha simulated  alpha expected
    smooth wave, constant intensity                   1.003 +- 0.010           1.000
    smooth wave, gamma-fluctuating (k = 4)            1.256 +- 0.012           1.250
    smooth wave, thermal light (k = 1)                1.974 +- 0.015           2.000
    single photons, one per window                    0.036 +- 0.002           0.039

Real experiments (typed in, from search summaries, not re-checked against the papers):
    1986 atomic cascade: classical bound alpha >= 1 violated by about 13 standard deviations;
    a modern teaching-laboratory down-conversion source: alpha = 0.0188 +- 0.0067
    (alpha equals the second-order coherence g2(0) of the light).
What the toy shows: the 'smooth wave + lumpy detector' picture explains the photoelectric effect (Lamb & Scully 1969:
a classical wave acting on quantum matter) but cannot make two detectors avoid clicking together; single photons can.
So the lumpiness is in the light itself.  Lamb and Scully's own claim was narrower: other effects, such as blackbody
radiation, Compton scattering and spontaneous emission, do need the quantised field (per search summaries).
A pilot-wave reading (de Broglie-Bohm) keeps a real wave AND a real particle riding on it: the closest standing
version of 'one is the light's property, the other what we meet when it interacts'; its predictions equal quantum theory's.

==============================================================================
B. Numbers behind the vase, the Planck energy and the black hole
==============================================================================
Vase (1 kg) turned entirely into energy, E = m c^2:
    8.988e+16 J = 21.5 megatons of TNT = 1,432 Hiroshima-size bombs.
    Turning pure energy back into matter makes equal amounts of matter and antimatter, not an ordinary vase, and the
    exact atom-by-atom arrangement is gone; gluing the pieces needs no such energy but cannot undo the heat and scatter.
Planck energy ('10^19'):
    E_P = sqrt(hbar c^5 / G) = 1.956e+09 J = 1.22e+19 GeV = 58 litres of petrol,
    9.0e+14 times one LHC collision (2.18e-06 J).  It is large per particle, not in joules:
    a 1 kg vase holds 4.6e+07 Planck energies, spread over about 1e26 atoms.
Black holes form by squeezing, not by a special energy: r_s = 2 G M / c^2.
    1 kg vase    M = 1.000e+00 kg  ->  squeeze to r_s = 1.485e-27 m
    Earth        M = 5.972e+24 kg  ->  squeeze to r_s = 8.870e-03 m (8.87 mm)
    Sun          M = 1.988e+30 kg  ->  squeeze to r_s = 2.953e+03 m (2.95 km)
    Planck mass  M = 2.176e-08 kg  ->  squeeze to r_s = 3.233e-35 m
Stiffness of space:
    c^4 / (8 pi G) = 4.82e+42 N (r03); the whole Sun bends starlight at its edge by only 1.751 arcseconds.
