W1. A source-free flow of a constant-sound-speed medium
==============================================================================
Constant sound speed: P = c^2 rho + const, so h(rho) = c^2 ln rho and Bernoulli reads v^2 / 2 + c^2 ln rho = c^2 ln rho_inf (flow at rest at infinity).  Continuity without sinks: r^2 rho v = q.
In units c = 1: rho = rho_inf exp(-v^2 / 2), mass flux through the sphere of radius r:  4 pi r^2 rho_inf v exp(-v^2/2).  The function v exp(-v^2/2) has its maximum at v = 1 (the sound speed):
    the flow is CHOKED: a given mass flux q can only pass through radii r >= r_c = sqrt(q e^(1/2) / (4 pi rho_inf)), where v reaches c exactly; inside r_c there is no steady source-free solution.
    So a horizon (v = c) exists only at the edge of the region where the sink sits; there is no free flow that goes through the sonic point to smaller radii.
    Source-free flow with its throat at r = r_h compared with the escape-speed profile c sqrt(r_h / r):
     r / r_h   v_free / c  v_escape / c  v_free / v_escape  rho / rho_inf
         1.0    1.000e+00     1.000e+00          1.000e+00        0.60653
         1.5    2.804e-01     8.165e-01          3.434e-01        0.96146
         2.0    1.534e-01     7.071e-01          2.170e-01        0.98830
         3.0    6.755e-02     5.774e-01          1.170e-01        0.99772

    => the source-free flow falls as 1/r^2 (incompressible sink), not as 1/sqrt(r): at 10 r_h it is already a factor ~ 50 below the escape speed and at 1000 r_h a factor ~ 5e4.  The escape-speed
       flow of gravity is NOT what the medium does by itself.

W2. What the escape-speed profile requires
==============================================================================
v = c sqrt(r_h / r), r_h = 2 G M / c^2, gravity g = G M / r^2 = c^2 r_h / (2 r^2).
  (a) Uniform density, no pressure gradient: Euler gives f = v dv/dr = -c^2 r_h / (2 r^2) (inward): a body force exactly equal to Newtonian gravity acting on the medium's own parcels (circular: it assumes gravity).
      Continuity: sigma = -(1/r^2) d(r^2 rho v)/dr = (3/2) rho v / r: a sink density in EVERY volume element, falling as r^(-3/2) (the total sink inside radius R grows as R^(3/2): it is not
      a localised source).
  (b) Constant sound speed, no sinks: continuity gives rho ~ r^(-3/2) (rho v r^2 = const); the pressure force per unit mass is -(1/rho) dP/dr = -c^2 d ln rho / dr = +(3/2) c^2 / r, directed OUTWARD, so the
      body force needed is f = g + (3/2) c^2 / r = g (1 + 3 r / r_h): larger than gravity by the factor 1 + 3 r / r_h:
         r / r_h  f / g (uniform)    f / g (constant c_s, no sink)
         1.5e+00              1.0                        5.500e+00
         3.0e+00              1.0                        1.000e+01
         1.0e+01              1.0                        3.100e+01
         1.0e+02              1.0                        3.010e+02
         1.0e+03              1.0                        3.001e+03
         1.0e+05              1.0                        3.000e+05
         1.0e+09              1.0                        3.000e+09
      At the surface of the Earth r / r_h = 7.18e+08 (r_h = 8.9 mm): the constant-sound-speed medium would need a body force 2.2e+09 times gravity.
  So the medium's own pressure cannot supply the infall (it opposes it), and the infall needs either a force as strong as gravity on the medium (circular) or a force far stronger.

W3. What the drain costs
==============================================================================
    Planck density 5.15e+96 kg/m^3.  Medium density rho = f rho_P: f = 0.7 (alpha = 1, n xi^3 = 1, r33) down to f = 7e-9 (the lowest value allowed by the string bound at formation, r42).

             Earth  6.37e+06    1.119e+04   5.70e+02              2.06e+115               2.06e+107                      3.45e+90

    The medium inside a volume of radius R is drained and replaced every free-fall time R / v (570 s at the Earth's surface, 30 min at the Sun's): the picture needs a steady inflow from large
    distances of an amount of medium per second that exceeds the object's own mass by a factor 1e90 (Earth, Sun) to 1e102 (galaxy) for a Planck-density medium.  Whether that is acceptable depends on what 'the medium is lost to the
    extra dimension' means for energy conservation; the numbers are the price of the kinematics.

W4. The area-energy half of the first law
==============================================================================
    Jacobson's argument needs the horizon area to grow when energy crosses it (Raychaudhuri).  In the flow picture the horizon is where v = c: r_h = 2 G M(< r_h) / c^2, so it responds to an energy flux dE
    exactly as in GR (delta r_h = 2 G dE / c^4) IF the flow obeys v^2 = -2 Phi with Phi sourced by the total energy (Poisson); then delta A = 8 pi r_h delta r_h and T_H = hbar c / (4 pi r_h k_B) (acoustic
    surface gravity kappa = c / (2 r_h) at the horizon, checked below) give delta E = T delta S with S = A / 4 l_P^2.
    Surface gravity of the escape flow (acoustic metric g_tt = -(c^2 - v^2)): kappa = c / (2 r_h); Hawking temperature T = hbar kappa / (2 pi k_B) = hbar c / (4 pi k_B r_h): in units hbar c / (k_B r_h) = 0.07958 (= 1/4pi = 0.07958).
    Check with M = 1e+30 kg, dE = 1e+20 J: r_h = 1.485e+03 m, T = 1.227e-07 K, delta S = 5.903e+49 k_B; T delta S / dE = 1.00000 (1 for the Bekenstein-Hawking first law).
    So the first law is satisfied exactly by a flow that obeys v^2 = -2 Phi with Poisson-sourced Phi, and by nothing in the fluid equations themselves: W1-W3 show the fluid does not produce that flow.  The geometric half
    of Einstein's equation is exactly the missing field equation for the flow (the 'source of the escape-speed flow').
