   ALWAYS carries a third, helicity-0 graviton that is either a ghost or unstable. At lambda = 1

E. Two ways out
==============================================================================
(i) healthy extension (M4): let the lapse vary in space with stiffness alpha (grad ln N)^2:
   lambda = 1.20, alpha = 0.5: omega^2/D^2 = +0.2308 (BPS analytic +0.2308) -> healthy
   lambda = 1.05, alpha = 0.1: omega^2/D^2 = +0.4419 (BPS analytic +0.4419) -> healthy
   lambda = 2.00, alpha = 1.5: omega^2/D^2 = +0.0667 (BPS analytic +0.0667) -> healthy
   lambda = 1.20, alpha = 2.5: omega^2/D^2 = -0.0154 (BPS analytic -0.0154) -> UNSTABLE
   healthy for lambda > 1, 0 < alpha < 2. But the extra scalar then exists and must be hidden:
   preferred-frame tests (|alpha_1| < ~4e-5 pulsars, |alpha_2| < ~2e-9 solar spin/pulsars; Shao & Wex
   2012, Shao et al. 2013) and GW170817 squeeze the extra couplings to the 1e-5 level or below
   (Gumrukcuoglu, Saravani & Sotiriou 2018).
(ii) the local Hamiltonian constraint (M5): exactly 2 helicity-2 modes, as observed. It says
   'the energy at each place is a constraint, not a clock': time reparametrisation is a gauge
   symmetry. A substrate with an absolute time does not have it automatically. It is the
   spacetime version of Page-Wootters (time = correlations; H Psi = 0) -- but LOCAL, at each cell.

==============================================================================
F. Out of the box: graviton = photon x photon (the double copy)
==============================================================================
Tree-level gravity amplitudes are squares of gauge amplitudes (Kawai-Lewellen-Tye 1986; Bern,
Carrasco & Johansson 2008-10). In polarisation language, twist (x) twist:
   photon +1 x photon +1: helicity +2
   photon +1 x photon -1: helicity +0
   photon -1 x photon +1: helicity +0
   photon -1 x photon -1: helicity -2
   => helicity +2, -2 (graviton) + two helicity-0 states (dilaton = trace, axion = antisymmetric).
   The dilaton couples to T like a scalar: it would make gamma != 1 and violate the equivalence
   principle unless massive. Torsion balances exclude a gravity-strength Yukawa beyond ~39 um
   (Lee et al., PRL 124, 101101 (2020)) -> dilaton mass > hbar c / 39 um = 5.1 meV.
   So 'gravity = light squared' fits the substrate idea (r02 gives the twist), but the extra
   scalars must be gapped, and it is a relation between amplitudes, not a construction.
