A. Kroener = Einstein: curvature of the strain is the density of defects
==============================================================================
linearised 3D Einstein tensor G_ij(h) = +1/2 inc(h)_ij, inc = eps_ikl eps_jmn d_k d_m h_ln:  True
   inc(eps) is exactly Kroener's incompatibility: in a crystal it equals the DEFECT DENSITY

weak-field mass (space part g_ij = (1 - 2 Phi) delta_ij, i.e. gamma = 1): spatial curvature R3 = 4 lap(Phi) = 16 pi G rho
=> a static mass is a cloud of disclination density in the substrate's strain, of strength
   16 pi G rho. This is precisely the SPATIAL half of the metric -- the 'mu' half that r08 needed
   (eps = mu) for the full light bending. The world-crystal picture supplies it automatically.

B. A wedge disclination lenses like a mass: double images
==============================================================================
Unfold the cone (deficit Omega): straight lines in the unfolded plane are the light rays.
   Omega = 60 deg, D_ls = 1.0, D_o = 1.0: image separation 30 deg; small-angle formula Omega D_ls/D_s = 30 deg
   Omega = 60 deg, D_ls = 1.0, D_o = 3.0: image separation 14.7385 deg; small-angle formula Omega D_ls/D_s = 15 deg
   Omega = 0.001 deg, D_ls = 1.0, D_o = 1.0: image separation 0.0005 deg; small-angle formula Omega D_ls/D_s = 0.0005 deg
   Omega = 0.001 deg, D_ls = 1.0, D_o = 3.0: image separation 0.00025 deg; small-angle formula Omega D_ls/D_s = 0.00025 deg
=> two images, separated by Omega D_ls/D_s for small Omega: identical to the cosmic-string lens
   (Vilenkin 1981, Gott 1985) with Omega = 8 pi G mu, and to a 2+1D point mass (Deser, Jackiw &
   't Hooft 1984) with Omega = 8 pi G m. Space around the defect is locally FLAT; the effect is
   global (missing angle) -- in 2+1D a point mass exerts no local force at all, only this.
   A negative disclination (7-fold, extra wedge) is an EXCESS angle: in 2+1D that is a negative
   mass, which defocuses -- the closest thing to 'antigravity' in this dictionary. Real crystals
   make +-disclinations in bound pairs (a pair = a dislocation).

C. Measured test of a CRYSTAL substrate: its disclination lines would be cosmic strings
==============================================================================
   cubic / FCC lattice (original USRT), 4-fold axis   Omega =  90.0 deg -> G mu = 0.0625 -> 4.2e+05 x the CMB bound
   hexagonal lattice, 6-fold axis                     Omega =  60.0 deg -> G mu = 0.0417 -> 2.8e+05 x the CMB bound
   cubic lattice, 3-fold [111] axis                   Omega = 120.0 deg -> G mu = 0.0833 -> 5.6e+05 x the CMB bound
CMB bound (Planck 2013 XXV): G mu < 1.5e-07  ->  allowed Frank angle < 3.8e-06 rad = 0.78 arcsec
   (pulsar-timing and LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA bounds are stronger still for most string-loop models)
=> a substrate with DISCRETE rotation symmetry would have quantised disclinations, each a cosmic
   string with a 60-120 degree deficit: one inside our horizon would double large parts of the
   sky. None is seen. So either the substrate has no free disclination lines anywhere in the
   visible universe (confined or never formed), or its rotation symmetry is CONTINUOUS at large
   scales -- a quantum liquid (crystal melted by dislocations, Zaanen et al.; or no lattice at all),
   where curvature can take any value, as GR requires. This independently agrees with the audit's
   verdict against a rigid crystal and with r06's 'no fixed lattice' option.

D. Brainstorm: what the defect picture adds, and its limits
==============================================================================
* Particles as defects (r07) and mass as defect density (A) are the SAME idea: trapped energy
  that cannot unwind is a topological defect; its gravitational field is the curvature it imposes.
* Dislocations = torsion: in the same dictionary the substrate's dislocations are torsion, which
  couples to spin (Einstein-Cartan; Hehl et al. 1976). Torsion is unobserved so far: another sign
  the substrate's dislocations must be condensed or absent at accessible scales.
* Fracton-elasticity duality (Pretko & Radzihovsky 2018): an isolated disclination cannot move
  (a 'fracton'); it moves only together with other defects. Pretko (2017) noted this gives a
  Mach-like inertia from the environment. Free particles DO move, so particles cannot be bare
  disclinations -- at most bound clusters.
* Limit: the dictionary gives the GEOMETRY of gravity (curvature = defects), not its DYNAMICS.
  Kleinert's world crystal needs a special 'second-gradient' elasticity for the defect fluctuations
  to produce the Einstein action; the graviton count and speed are again the questions of r12-r13.
