==============================================================================
B. If the medium's healing length is a Planck length (c_s = c)
==============================================================================
xi = hbar / (sqrt(2) m c) fixes the constituent mass, m = m_P / (sqrt(2) alpha) for xi = alpha l_P:
    alpha = 1: xi = 1.62e-35 m, m = 1.54e-08 kg = 8.63e+18 GeV
    alpha = 5: xi = 8.08e-35 m, m = 3.08e-09 kg = 1.73e+18 GeV
Superluminal dispersion, v_g / c - 1 = (3/4)(k xi)^2: delay of a 10 TeV photon over 1 Gpc,
          xi (m)    delay (s)
        1.62e-35     5.18e-14
        1.00e-30     1.98e-04
        1.00e-28     1.98e+00
        1.00e-27     1.98e+02
        1.00e-26     1.98e+04
    a timing precision of 1 s probes xi ~ 7.1e-29 m and 100 s probes xi ~ 7.1e-28 m; a Planck-length medium
    (xi = 1.62e-35 m) gives 5.2e-14 s, 2e+13 times below the 1 s sensitivity.
    A medium that is NOT Planck-sized is testable: xi above ~1e-28 m would already be visible in TeV timing.
    Not tested here: the matter particles' own dispersion. In He-3 each species has its own 'speed of light' and
    making them all equal to 1e-15 is a fine-tuning (E9, open problem 2); a single superfluid does not provide it.
