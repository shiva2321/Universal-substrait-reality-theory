==============================================================================
cell size a                                               a (m)   coupling J = hbar c/2a   tick a/c (s)

largest allowed by order-2 dispersion bound            1.97e-27          5.00e+10 GeV       6.58e-36
A twist-only (gauge) lattice is symmetric under k -> -k, so its leading dispersion is order 2
and both polarisations stay degenerate at every k (r02): no vacuum birefringence. Data therefore
allow cells up to ~2e-27 m; gravity's strength (Sakharov) points to a few Planck lengths.
cells in the observable universe if a = l_P: (R/a)^3 = 2.0e+184

==============================================================================
2. Is the substrate in the phase that has photons? (Coulomb vs confined)
==============================================================================
simplest lattice gauge substrate (compact U(1), Wilson action): photons exist only for
  beta = 1/e^2 > 1.0111, i.e. alpha = e^2/(4 pi) < alpha_c = 0.0787  (about 1/12.7)
our alpha(0) = 1/137.036 is 10.8x below the threshold; alpha(M_Z) = 1/127.95 is 10.1x below
=> the substrate must sit in its Coulomb (deconfined) phase, comfortably. Quantum spin ice's emergent
   alpha ~ 0.08 (Pace et al. 2021) sits right at that threshold; ours is an order of magnitude weaker.
   Why 1/137 exactly is still open.

==============================================================================
3. Vacuum energy
==============================================================================
observed dark-energy density (Planck 2018): 5.84e-27 kg/m^3 = 5.24e-10 J/m^3
Planck density c^5/(hbar G^2): 5.15e+96 kg/m^3 -> ratio 1.1e-123
Volovik (Gibbs-Duhem): a substrate in equilibrium has zero vacuum pressure, so the naive 1e120 term
is absent. The small observed value then has to come from the universe being slightly out of
equilibrium; its size is still an input, not derived.
