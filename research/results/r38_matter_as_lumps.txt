B. Vortex rings
==============================================================================
Large-ring asymptotics (units hbar = m = n0 = 1, kappa = 2 pi, c = 1):
         R        v          E          P  E / (c P)  E / (P v)
         4   0.3082      115.7      315.8     0.3664      1.189
         8   0.1974      340.9     1263.3     0.2699      1.367
        16   0.1204      900.7     5053.2     0.1783      1.481
        40   0.0596     2975.3    31582.7     0.0942      1.581
       100   0.0284     9247.1   197392.1     0.0468      1.648
       400   0.0088    47934.0  3158273.4     0.0152      1.717
    (R below ~4 core sizes is outside the asymptotic formulas and only qualitatively right.)
    => E/(cP) = 2 (v/c)(ln - 2)/(ln - 1), always below 2v/c: every ring has E < cP (impossible for a particle with a real rest mass); E/(Pv) -> 2 where a particle
       with rest mass has c^2/v^2 -> infinity as v -> 0; and as R grows the ring slows (v -> 0) while E ~ R ln R and P ~ R^2 grow without limit:
       there is no state at rest with a finite energy, so no rest mass.  As R shrinks to the core size the ring becomes a sound pulse (E -> cP).

C. A cavity (bubble) in the medium
==============================================================================
Spherically symmetric 3D GP, hole of depth 90 % and Gaussian width w; deficit of particles inside r < w against time.
        w  t_half (c/w)   t_half   central density at t = w/c  energy drift
      2.0         0.800     1.60                        1.167       2.0e-03
      4.0         0.850     3.40                        1.105       9.6e-04
      8.0         0.875     7.00                        1.057       5.3e-04
    => the deficit inside the original size halves in t = 0.84 w/c for every size: a cavity
       of size w lives about w/c, then its emptiness leaves as a sound pulse.  A 1 mm cavity would last 3 ps; a Planck-size one a few Planck times.
       Only a source that keeps pushing (a repulsive impurity, E1, r01) holds it open, and then the dent is screened (short range).
