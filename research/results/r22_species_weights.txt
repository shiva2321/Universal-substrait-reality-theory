Dirac fermion, Wilson r = 4.0 (doubler masses 8, 16, 24): eta_D = 0.04327  ->  counts as 1.79 real scalars
   box check (r = 1, 40^3 box): eta_D = 0.22076 (32^3: 0.22075) -- converged
=> a Dirac fermion's weight falls steadily with r (16.5, 9.1, 4.3, 1.8 scalars for r = 0.5, 1, 2, 4):
   light doublers add UV entanglement, and a large Wilson term also stiffens the physical branch. There
   is no plateau at 'one per degree of freedom': the weight is a property of the substrate's fine structure.

==============================================================================
C. The Standard Model's effective species number, and what the observed Lambda implies
==============================================================================
N_eff = 4 x 1 (Higgs) + 22.5 x w_D (fermions) + 12 x w_V (gauge fields)
w_V is not computed here: gauge-field entanglement depends on how the edge (boundary) modes are
treated -- each polarisation like a scalar gives w_V = 2; the heat-kernel contact term (Kabat 1995;
Donnelly & Wall, PRL 114, 111603 (2015)) can make it negative. We scan w_V from -4 to +2.
   r = 0.5: N_eff =   327 ...   399   (Lambda needs 180-372) -> overlaps
   r = 1.0: N_eff =   162 ...   234   (Lambda needs 180-372) -> overlaps
   r = 2.0: N_eff =    53 ...   125   (Lambda needs 180-372) -> misses
   r = 4.0: N_eff =    -4 ...    68   (Lambda needs 180-372) -> misses
inverting: today's Lambda needs a fermion weight w_D between 6.8 and 18.5 scalars
   -> for Wilson-type fermions: r <~ 1.3 (r = 0.5 still allowed; below it not computed)
=> the observed Lambda is CONSISTENT with the Standard Model's particle content on this substrate for
   the standard regulator (r = 1 lies inside r <~ 1.3) -- not a fine-tuned coincidence. Read the
   other way, R6 turns the cosmological constant into a MEASUREMENT of the substrate's fine structure.
   Still open: the gauge-field weight (edge modes), and the dynamics that fixes the early-universe
   problem of E29.
