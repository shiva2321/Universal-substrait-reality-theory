C. Jacobson / Bekenstein-Hawking: which cell size gives the measured G?
==============================================================================
Bekenstein-Hawking: S = A / (4 l_P^2). Substrate with N scalar fields: S = N eta_a A / a^2.
Setting them equal (Jacobson 1995: then Einstein's equations follow): a = 2 sqrt(N eta_a) l_P.
   entanglement, cubic lattice:   a = 0.311 sqrt(N) l_P
   entanglement, radial lattice:  a = 0.307 sqrt(N) l_P
   Sakharov (r13), from inertia:  a = 0.364 sqrt(N) l_P
   Sakharov (r13), from stiffness: a = 0.261 sqrt(N) l_P
=> two completely different quantum calculations -- how much the vacuum RESISTS straining
   (Sakharov) and how much it is ENTANGLED across a surface (Bekenstein-Hawking/Jacobson) --
   give the same cell size to within 17%: cells of ~0.3 sqrt(N) Planck lengths.
   With a Lorentz-invariant cutoff the two agree EXACTLY: S_ent = A / (4 G_induced) (Susskind &
   Uglum 1994; Jacobson 1994). The lattice spread is the same rest-frame effect found in r13.
   (Noticed after the fact, one comparison tried: eta_a = 0.0242 is within 2% of the geometric mean
    of the two Sakharov values of 1/(4G). Possibly a coincidence; not derived.)
   N =   1 fields: a = 0.31 l_P = 5.0e-36 m
   N =  10 fields: a = 0.98 l_P = 1.6e-35 m
   N = 100 fields: a = 3.11 l_P = 5.0e-35 m

==============================================================================
D. What this says about 'gravity and quantum mechanics are one thing at different scales'
==============================================================================
* Gravity's strength G is NOT an independent constant here. It is fixed by quantum quantities of
  the substrate: the number of fields N, the cell size a, and hbar:  G ~ a^2 c^3 / (4 N eta_a hbar).
  The Planck length l_P = sqrt(hbar G / c^3) is then ~3.2 x (cell size) / sqrt(N).
* Black-hole entropy A/4 l_P^2 becomes the entanglement of the substrate across the horizon,
  counted with the same fields that set G: one consistent quantum origin for both.
* Einstein's equations become an equation of state: the thermodynamics of substrate entanglement
  (Jacobson 1995). Gravity is then quantum mechanics of the substrate seen at large scales,
  like elasticity is the quantum mechanics of atoms seen at large scales.
* What this does NOT give: the graviton's quantum dynamics at the Planck scale, and why the
  emergent equations are exactly Einstein's (Lorentz invariance again: r12, r13).
