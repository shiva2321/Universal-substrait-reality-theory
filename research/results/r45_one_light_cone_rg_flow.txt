Q4. What would reach 1e-15, and the QED-like case
==============================================================================
    Fixed coupling: the speeds agree to 1e-15 at 1 TeV only if lambda = g^2/(16 pi^2) = 0.20 (g^2 = 31) all the way from the Planck scale.
    (linear estimate: lambda = ln(d_0 / 1e-15) / (4.67 x 37.0) = 0.20.)
    Running coupling: the speed difference falls by exp(-4.67 * (1/10) ln(1 + 10 lambda_0 l)); reaching 1e-15 needs int lambda dl = 7.4, i.e. lambda_0 = 5.6e+29:
    impossible: for lambda_0 = 1 (strong already) the integral is 0.59.
    QED-like case (alpha = 1/137): photon-side rate e^2/(12 pi^2) = 7.74e-04 per e-fold; counting the fermion side as equal, an O(1) mismatch at the Planck scale is reduced only by exp(-0.057) = 0.944
    by 1 TeV: the standard-model-size flow does essentially nothing (Collins et al. 2004: induced violations 1e-3-1e-2 survive).
    Conclusion: in the infrared-free, weakly coupled regime the Lorentz-invariant point is attractive but the approach is a power of the energy with exponent ~ 1e-2 (g^2/16pi^2 ~ 1e-3-1e-2) or only a logarithm
    when the coupling itself runs to zero: 15 digits of agreement cannot come from the flow; they need either a symmetry that forbids the difference or a strongly coupled UV fixed point (Bednik, Pujolas & Sibiryakov 2013).
