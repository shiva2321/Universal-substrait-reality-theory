B. Continuum: a Lorentz-invariant regulator gives c_g = c and Sakharov's G
==============================================================================
hard momentum cutoff Lambda = 1: M = 1.0000 Lambda^2/(480 pi^2),  beta = 1.0000 x 11 Lambda^2/(840 pi^2)
   -> c_g^2 = beta/(4M) = 1.57143 = 11/7: the graviton would be 1.254 c
Pauli-Villars (masses 0, mu, sqrt2 mu; weights 1, -2, 1): M = 7.3156763e-04 mu^2, Sakharov heat kernel mu^2 ln2/(96 pi^2) = 7.3156763e-04
   -> c_g^2 = 0.999999994
=> with a Lorentz-invariant cutoff the induced graviton moves at exactly c and the induced G is
   Sakharov's. With a cutoff that singles out a rest frame, c_g is off by O(1).

==============================================================================
D. Can the substrate protect c_g = c? A Lorentz-invariant cutoff below the cell scale
==============================================================================
Add to the lattice two regulator fields of mass mu and sqrt2 mu with weights -2, +1 (the Pauli-
Villars pattern). They are FORMAL: negative weight means they are not ordinary particles. They
stand for any mechanism that cuts the fluctuations off Lorentz-invariantly at a scale mu below
the cell scale 1/a. (Ordinary fermions do not do this: they ADD to 1/G like ~2 scalars each.)
   mu a = 0.80: c_g^2 - 1 = -2.112e-01,  M / Sakharov = 1.1283
   mu a = 0.40: c_g^2 - 1 = -9.875e-02,  M / Sakharov = 1.1083
   mu a = 0.20: c_g^2 - 1 = -3.705e-02,  M / Sakharov = 1.0465
   mu a = 0.10: c_g^2 - 1 = -1.213e-02,  M / Sakharov = 1.0162
   mu a = 0.05: c_g^2 - 1 = -3.699e-03,  M / Sakharov = 1.0052
   fit (mu a <= 0.2): c_g^2 - 1 = -0.411 (mu a)^2 ln(1.91/(mu a))  [the (scale ratio)^2
   suppression expected when Lorentz violation enters only through higher-dimension operators:
   Groot Nibbelink & Pospelov, PRL 94, 081601 (2005) (via supersymmetry); Polchinski, CQG 29,
   088001 (2012)]
   GW170817 (|c_g^2 - 1| < 6e-15) needs mu a < 2.8e-08 (extrapolating the fitted form
   from mu a = 0.05 down -- an assumption, not a computation, over those 6-7 decades)
   N =   1: measured G needs partner mass mu = 5.21 m_P -> cells a < 5.5e-09 l_P
   N =  10: measured G needs partner mass mu = 1.65 m_P -> cells a < 1.7e-08 l_P
   N = 100: measured G needs partner mass mu = 0.52 m_P -> cells a < 5.5e-08 l_P
=> a substrate CAN induce gravity with c_g = c, but only if (i) something cuts its fluctuations
   off Lorentz-invariantly (what physical mechanism does this is OPEN), and (ii) its cells are
   ~10^7-10^8 times SMALLER than the Planck length. The Planck scale is then set by mu, not by
   the cell size, and the r10 lattice signatures (TeV photon delays) become unobservable.
