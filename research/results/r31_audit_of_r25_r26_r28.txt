A. r25 (R15): the causal-horizon memory kernel
==============================================================================
A1. Poisson additivity: split a Poisson count (mean 1e6 elements) into K independent patches, add the patch
    counts, and measure the relative fluctuation of the total in units of the unsplit value 1/sqrt(1e6).
    r25 multiplies the monolithic noise by 1/sqrt(K) (its 'central limit theorem' step); additivity says 1.
    K =     1 patches: measured 1.000 +- 0.000   r25's rule 1.000
    K =     4 patches: measured 1.000 +- 0.001   r25's rule 0.500
    K =    16 patches: measured 1.000 +- 0.001   r25's rule 0.250
    K =    64 patches: measured 0.994 +- 0.003   r25's rule 0.125
    K =   256 patches: measured 0.998 +- 0.006   r25's rule 0.062
    K =  1024 patches: measured 0.995 +- 0.011   r25's rule 0.031
    K =  4096 patches: measured 1.019 +- 0.022   r25's rule 0.016
    => consistent with 1 for every K (largest deviation 2.1 sigma among 7 points).  This is the
       additivity of independent Poisson variables made visible: disjoint counts are already independent, adding
       them in quadrature returns sqrt(V) exactly, so the extra 1/sqrt(N_patches) in r25 counts the averaging
       twice. (A separate Lambda per patch would make the noise larger, not smaller.)

A2. Which observer?  r25 takes d_C(a) = comoving distance from the epoch a to TODAY (line 69-74) and applies
    it to the noise AT epoch a.  For an observer living at the epoch, the cone is built from emission epochs
    a_em < a_obs and the patch count is N(a_em; a_obs) = 1 + 4 [(eta_obs - eta_em) a_em H_em]^2.
    check: exact V(z=1e9) / (8 pi t^4 / 105) = 1.00000;  V_today = 0.12287 (r25: 0.12287, r21: 0.1229) in (c/H0)^4
    needed at BBN (E29 design target): suppression <= 1/43 = 0.0233
    epoch                   as coded 1/sqrt(N)  observer at epoch  N_median  N_horizon
    BBN, z = 1e9                     1.639e-08              0.537       3.5        5.0
    recombination, z = 1100           7.490e-03              0.522       3.7        8.2
    z = 10                           1.228e-01              0.509       3.9       15.4
    z = 1                            5.898e-01              0.508       3.9       19.7
    today, z = 0                     1.000e+00              0.506       3.9       41.4
    'observer at epoch' = 1/sqrt(N_median), N_median = volume-weighted median patch count across the observer's own
    cone; N_horizon = 1 + 4 (eta a H)^2 at the observer's particle horizon.  E41 gives the exact values 5 (radiation era,
    eta a H = 1) and 17 (pure matter era, eta a H = 2); the row z = 1e9 reproduces 5, the rows z = 1100 and 10 approach
    17, and the count grows as the cosmological constant takes over (about 41 today).  Either way it is O(1-50), never 1e15.

A3. Raw (white-noise) |Omega_Lambda| at BBN.  r25 line 88 floors the cone volume: V = max(1e-25, V).
    z = 1e9: exact cone volume 1.766e-66, r25 uses 1.000e-25 (the floor)
    raw rms |Omega_Lambda| at BBN: exact 1.96 (r21 and E29: 1.96);  r25 prints 0.0000
    r25's volume is off by more than 10 % for every z above 7.6e+03
    => the headline 'filtered |Omega_Lambda| = 1.4e-28 at BBN' is an artefact. With the exact volume the same
       kernel gives 3.2e-08: still 'passes', but only because of the frame problem in A2.

A4. Equation of state and dark-energy density of r25's rms envelope (what r25 plots against DESI).
    r25 envelope: w0 = -0.343  (r25 prints -0.343)   rho_DE(z=1) / rho_DE(0) = 3.12
    DESI+CMB+Pantheon+     w0 = -0.838 +- 0.055  -> w0 is 9.0 sigma from r25's envelope; CPL best fit gives rho_DE(z=1)/rho_DE(0) = 0.98
    DESI+CMB+Union3        w0 = -0.667 +- 0.088  -> w0 is 3.7 sigma from r25's envelope; CPL best fit gives rho_DE(z=1)/rho_DE(0) = 1.06
    r25's text says 'produces w0 ~ -1, consistent with Planck 2018 and DESI DR2' (line 190, a fixed string).
    No history is evolved at late times in r25: the 100,000 'histories' draw independent Gaussians at three epochs.

C. r28 (R17): neutral Hopfion dark matter
==============================================================================
C1. r28 line 89: M_sol = RATIO_OBS * m_p = 5.3878 * 0.9383 = 5.0552 GeV.  The 'predicted'
    mass is the observed Omega_c / Omega_b restated, under the assumption n_sol = n_b (asymmetric-dark-matter
    reasoning, Nussinov 1985; Kaplan, Luty & Zurek 2009).  Then F = M / 312, R = hbar c / F: nothing independent.
    F_sub = 16.20 MeV, R_sol = 12.18 fm.

C2. Cross section.  r28 line 53 computes sigma = pi R^2 = 4.660e-24 cm^2 -> sigma/M = 0.517 cm^2/g.
    E38 states 'sigma = 4 pi R^2' (the number 4.660e-24 it quotes is pi R^2).  With 4 pi R^2: sigma/M = 2.068 cm^2/g,
    above the 1.0 cm^2/g limit that E38 itself uses (BULLET_CLUSTER_MAX, r28 line 40).  For two hard spheres of radius R the
    classical cross section is 4 pi R^2; pi R^2 is a point particle on one sphere.
    Not computed: sigma_SI = 1e-49 cm^2 is a typed number (r28 line 210), the exclusion curve is a hand-drawn function
    (line 201) and disagrees with LZ_LIMIT_5GEV in the same file.
    (Merging-cluster analyses quote limits nearer 0.5 cm^2/g, e.g. Harvey et al. 2015; from memory, not re-checked.)

C3. What the 'sweet spot' needs.  sigma/M scales as M^-3 at fixed form factor, so it is set by the assumption n_sol / n_b:
     n_sol / n_b   M (GeV)     pi R^2   4 pi R^2   (cm^2/g; band 0.1 - 1.0)
             0.5     10.11      0.065      0.259
             1.0      5.06      0.517      2.068
             2.0      2.53      4.137     16.546
    band reached for M in [4.1, 8.7] GeV with pi R^2 (n_sol / n_b in [0.58, 1.25]) and
    for M in [6.4, 13.9] GeV with 4 pi R^2 (n_sol / n_b in [0.36, 0.78]): n_sol = n_b falls
    outside the window for the convention E38 states.
    Neither the 312 F/e energy coefficient nor the Hopfion size is computed in this repository, and the F = 16 MeV scale
    is unrelated to the Planck-scale causal-set substrate of v2.3 (cells of ~5 l_P).

C4. Scaling with the Skyrme coupling e at fixed mass.  r28 uses F = M e / 312 and R = hbar c / (e F) = 312 hbar c / (e^2 M),
    so R goes as e^-2 and sigma/M as e^-4.  E41 and the README row state e^-2.
    e = 0.50: R =  48.72 fm, sigma/M =   8.273 (pi R^2) or  33.093 (4 pi R^2) cm^2/g;  ratio to e = 1: 16.000  (e^-4 = 16.000, e^-2 =  4.000)
    e = 0.70: R =  24.85 fm, sigma/M =   2.154 (pi R^2) or   8.614 (4 pi R^2) cm^2/g;  ratio to e = 1:  4.165  (e^-4 =  4.165, e^-2 =  2.041)
    e = 1.00: R =  12.18 fm, sigma/M =   0.517 (pi R^2) or   2.068 (4 pi R^2) cm^2/g;  ratio to e = 1:  1.000  (e^-4 =  1.000, e^-2 =  1.000)
    e = 1.40: R =   6.21 fm, sigma/M =   0.135 (pi R^2) or   0.538 (4 pi R^2) cm^2/g;  ratio to e = 1:  0.260  (e^-4 =  0.260, e^-2 =  0.510)
    e = 2.00: R =   3.04 fm, sigma/M =   0.032 (pi R^2) or   0.129 (4 pi R^2) cm^2/g;  ratio to e = 1:  0.062  (e^-4 =  0.062, e^-2 =  0.250)
    Under either cross-section convention the value at e = 1 is not 'safely inside' a band: a factor 2 in e moves it by 16.
